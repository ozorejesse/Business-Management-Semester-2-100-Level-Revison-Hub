1. Forms of Business Ownership

Covers key legal structures, criteria for selection, liability profiles, taxation structures, and special entities like social enterprises.

Key Decision Factors

  • Evaluation Criteria: Tax considerations, liability exposure, capital requirements (startup & future), management control, managerial ability, vision, succession planning, and formation costs.
  • Sole Proprietorship: Owned/managed by one person. Riskiest due to unlimited personal liability. Simple setup, total decision authority, direct profit incentive, marginal tax rates (7.5% - 15%). Limited capital and lack of continuity.
  • Partnerships: Co-owned by two or more people. Shared capital and complementary skills.
    • General Partners: Active management role, unlimited personal liability. Every partnership must have at least one.
    • Limited Partners: Cannot manage day-to-day operations; limited liability. Includes Silent Partners (known to public) and Dormant Partners (unknown to public).
    • LLP (Limited Liability Partnership): Limits all partners' liability; pass-through income tax (no entity-level tax).
  • Corporation & Limited Companies: Separate legal entity from owners.
    • Publicly Held (PLC): Traded on stock exchanges, minimum 2 shareholders, minimum 2 directors, raises capital via public share sales. Subject to double taxation.
    • Closely Held (Ltd): Shares owned by small group (e.g., family), restrictions on share transfers.
    • LLC (Limited Liability Company): Combines limited liability of a corporation with pass-through taxation of a partnership. Requires Articles of Organization (name, address, duration) and an Operating Agreement (provisions governing conduct).
  • Social Enterprise: Uses commercial strategies to achieve social/environmental benefits. Profits are primarily reinvested rather than distributed. Focuses on self-generated revenue over grants/donations.
LLC Core Docs = Articles of Organization + Operating Agreement
PLC Structure = Board of Directors (Elected) + Shareholders (Limited Liability) + Stock Sales

2. Production Function & Management

Details manufacturing processes, operations strategies, aids to production, and modern Industry 4.0 innovations.

Key Concepts & Production Systems

  • Production Process Types:
    • Jobbing / Job Shop: Custom, customized, one-off or small-batch items. Labor-intensive, skilled workforce, flexible layout, general-purpose machinery (e.g., tailoring, specialty printing).
    • Project-Based: Temporary teams assembled around unique projects with fixed scopes, timelines, and budgets (e.g., construction, film production, software engineering).
    • Batch Production: Standardized parts produced in discrete lots/batches. Middle ground between jobbing and mass production; emphasizes planning, scheduling, and general-purpose machines.
    • Mass & Continuous Production: High-volume, highly automated, standardized production lines. Low per-unit cost, rigid specifications, specialized machines (e.g., automotive, smartphones).
  • Aids to Production & Engineering:
    • Work Study: Method study and work measurement to streamline operational workflows.
    • Value Engineering (VE): Systematic process to maximize functionality/quality while minimizing overall lifecycle costs.
    • Value Analysis (VA): Application of VE focused specifically on analyzing existing component parts, materials, and designs.
    • Quality Circles: Small, voluntary employee groups meeting regularly to analyze data and solve quality problems.
  • Modern Manufacturing Tech: CAD/CAM, ERP systems, Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing), Cobots (collaborative robots), AR/VR, IoT, and AI-driven automation.
Value Optimization = Maximizing Functionality & Quality ÷ Minimizing Lifecycle Costs
Batch vs. Jobbing = Standardized Lots vs. Fully Custom/Single Units

3. Marketing & Market Research

Covers definitions of marketing vs. selling, marketing orientations, the 7 Ps mix, bootstrap tactics, and market research methodologies.

Key Concepts & Breakdown

  • Marketing vs. Selling: Marketing creates, delivers, and exchanges value to satisfy target needs at a profit. Selling is assisting or persuading a customer to buy an existing product.
  • Orientations: Production, Product, Sales, Market (customer-centric), and Societal orientations.
  • The Marketing Mix (7 Ps):
    • Traditional 4 Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion.
    • Extended 3 Ps (Services): People, Process, Physical Evidence.
  • Bootstrap Marketing: Unconventional, low-cost, creative marketing strategies for small businesses to maximize ROI without large budgets. Focuses on finding niches, building community, publicity, and emotional connection.
  • Market vs. Marketing Research:
    • Market Research: Focuses on market size, target demographics, trends, segments, and demand.
    • Marketing Research: Broader scope evaluating ad effectiveness, concept testing, promotional methods, copywriting, and customer satisfaction.
  • USP & Brand Attributes: Unique Selling Proposition. Uses Threshold Attributes (expected baseline), Performance Attributes (superior features), and Excitement Attributes (unexpected differentiators).
Extended Mix = 4 Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) + 3 Ps (People, Process, Physical Evidence)
USP Triad = Threshold Attributes + Performance Attributes + Excitement Attributes

4. Managerial Competencies & Stress Management

Analyzes manager development, talent management, stress symptoms/interventions, employee counseling, job evaluation, and employee relations.

Key Concepts & Frameworks

  • Managerial Competencies & Development: Clusters of Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities (KSAs). Key roles: Self skills, Providing direction, Facilitating change, Working with people, Using resources, Achieving results.
    • Development Methods: CPD (Continuing Professional Development), Coaching, Job Rotation, Delegation, Secondments, Case Studies, and Simulations.
    • Succession Planning & Talent Management: Identifying high-potential employees to fill leadership roles, ensuring continuity and knowledge retention.
  • Workplace Stress: Adverse physical/psychological reactions to demands exceeding individual coping capacity.
    • Causes: Heavy workload, role ambiguity, lack of control, poor relationships/bullying, organizational changes.
    • Symptoms: Physiological (hypertension, ulcers), Psychological (anxiety, depression), Behavioral (absenteeism, substance abuse).
  • Counseling & Harassment:
    • Counseling: Professional guidance providing active listening, confidentiality, root cause identification, and joint action planning.
    • Harassment & Discrimination: Unwelcome conduct targeting protected characteristics (race, gender, age, religion, disability). Employer must establish reporting, investigate, and take corrective action.
  • Job Evaluation & Employee Relations:
    • Job Evaluation Methods: Analytical (Points rating, Factor comparison) vs. Non-analytical (Job ranking, Job classification). Used to establish internal equity and salary structures.
    • Employee Relations: Strategic management of individual/collective workplace relationships, aiming for open communication, fairness, engagement, and constructive conflict resolution.
Job Evaluation Types = Analytical (Points, Factors) vs. Non-Analytical (Ranking, Grading)
Managerial KSAs = Knowledge + Skills + Abilities
