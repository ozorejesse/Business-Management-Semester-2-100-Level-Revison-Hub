Week 1: Marketing Function, Environment and Marketing Mix

Covers the foundational marketing concept, overarching goals, exchange mechanics, detailed functions, internal/external environments, and the core 4 Ps marketing mix[cite: 1].

Key Concepts & Breakdown

  • Marketing Concept: The process of getting appropriate goods, services, or ideas to target audiences at the right place, time, and price, using proper promotion and suitable employees[cite: 1].
  • Goals of Marketing: Creation of utility, cost reduction, price stability, maximizing customer satisfaction, maximizing quality of life, and maximizing choice[cite: 1].
  • Exchange Mechanics: Occurs when two parties give something of value to each other to satisfy respective needs or wants[cite: 1].
  • Marketing Functions: Divided into Exchange Functions (buying/selling activities)[cite: 1], Physical Supply Functions (transportation & storage)[cite: 1], and Facilitating Functions (financing, risk-taking, market information, standardization & grading, pricing, branding, packaging, salesmanship, advertising)[cite: 1].
  • Marketing Environment Forces: Internal controllable forces (resources like 7Ms: men, money, machine, material, minute, media, margin)[cite: 1], Micro partially-controllable forces (customers, competitors, suppliers, intermediaries, publics)[cite: 1], and Macro uncontrollable forces (demographic, economic, socio-cultural, technological, ecological, political/legal, competitive)[cite: 1].
  • The 4 Ps Marketing Mix: Product (brand name, packaging, image), Price (delivering fair customer value), Place (shipping, channels, stores), and Promotion (communicating product benefits)[cite: 1].
Marketing Focus Matrix: Target Market ➔ Consumer Need ➔ Integrated Marketing ➔ Profit via Satisfaction[cite: 1]
Internal Resources (7 Ms): Men + Money + Machine + Material + Minute + Media + Margin[cite: 1]

Week 2: Product Planning and Marketing Mix in Details

Explores internal product planning processes, product attributes, product life cycle stages, elastic vs inelastic pricing demand, promotion mix components, salesmanship, and channel distribution levels[cite: 2].

Key Concepts & Breakdown

  • Product Planning & Dimensions: Decisions to develop and release products at optimal times to meet needs and maximize profit[cite: 2]. Includes Managerial, Consumer, and Societal dimensions[cite: 2].
  • Product Attributes: Physical features, utilities, brand/package/label, design (color, shape, size), price, service, company image, and safety[cite: 2].
  • Product Life Cycle (PLC): 6 key stages: Development ➔ Introduction ➔ Growth ➔ Maturity ➔ Saturation ➔ Decline[cite: 2]. Innovation and product improvements can alter the cycle[cite: 2].
  • Necessities & Maturity: Products like salt and water stay in maturity indefinitely because they are necessities with inelastic demand and lack close substitutes[cite: 2].
  • Promotion Mix: Advertising (mass paid non-personal), Publicity (unpaid news stimulation), Personal Selling (face-to-face interaction), and Sales Promotion (short-term incentives, displays, shows)[cite: 2].
  • Salesmanship: Direct personal contact inducing/assisting buyers[cite: 2]. Viewed as an Art (study/skills), Science (systematized theory), and Profession (proficient code of conduct)[cite: 2].
PLC Curve: Development ➔ Introduction ➔ Growth ➔ Maturity ➔ Saturation ➔ Decline[cite: 2]
Economist Price Formula: Money (Price) = Bundle of Perceived Expectations / Satisfaction[cite: 2]

Week 3: Distribution Channels in Supply Chain

Examines direct and indirect multi-tiered channel architectures, distributor roles to end-consumers, retailers, and OEMs, as well as critical selection criteria for distribution design[cite: 3].

Key Concepts & Breakdown

  • Distribution vs Physical Distribution: Distribution gets the right product to the right customer at the right place and time[cite: 3]. Physical distribution is the actual movement along that path[cite: 3].
  • Channel Structures: Direct (supplier to customer directly)[cite: 3], One-Tier (uses 1 intermediary e.g. overseas agents)[cite: 3], Two-Tier (manufacturer to cash & carry wholesaler to kiosk to customer)[cite: 3], Multiple-Tier (multiple dealers e.g. single cigarette/sachet soap sales)[cite: 3], and OEM Channels (supplier components embedded in another manufacturer's finished product)[cite: 3].
  • Distributor Roles to Retailers: Demand Generation (channel financing, marketing funds, training)[cite: 3], Supply Fulfillment (bulk breaking, outbound/reverse logistics)[cite: 3], Market Intelligence (sales-out reporting)[cite: 3], and Outsourced Services (warranty management)[cite: 3].
  • Channel Choice Criteria: Product characteristics (perishable vs durable, unit value)[cite: 3], Market factors (size, industrial vs consumer)[cite: 3], Middlemen cooperation[cite: 3], Company financial strength[cite: 3], and Environmental/Economic conditions (recession favors shorter channels)[cite: 3].
One-Tier = Manufacturer ➔ Intermediary/Retailer ➔ Consumer[cite: 3]
Two-Tier = Manufacturer ➔ Wholesaler/Cash & Carry ➔ Retailer ➔ Consumer[cite: 3]

Week 4: Production and Operations Management

Analyzes production systems, historic contributors to scientific management, strategic/operational/control decision tiers, product/process design types, and technology selection factors[cite: 4].

Key Concepts & Breakdown

  • Production System Components: Production System (overall input-output conversion)[cite: 4], Conversion Sub-system (transformation mechanics)[cite: 4], and Control Sub-system (monitoring outputs for corrective feedback)[cite: 4].
  • Key Pioneers of Scientific Management:
    • F.W. Taylor: Scientific management principles, exception principle, time study[cite: 4].
    • Frank & Lilian Gilbreth: Motion study, fatigue studies, human factors, employee selection[cite: 4].
    • Henry L. Gantt: Gantt charts, incentive pay systems, humanistic labor training[cite: 4].
    • Carl G. Barth: Mathematical analysis, slide rules, feeds & speeds studies[cite: 4].
  • Production Decision Tiers: Strategic (long-term facility location, technology selection)[cite: 4], Operating (aggregate planning, scheduling, inventory control)[cite: 4], and Control Decisions (quality control, human efficiency, maintenance)[cite: 4].
  • Product & Process Design: Functional Design (performance capability)[cite: 4], Form Design (aesthetics/appearance)[cite: 4], Process Design (overall operational sequence)[cite: 4], and Production Design (manufacturability/producibility focus)[cite: 4].
  • Technology Selection: Driven internally by available funds and plant capacity[cite: 4]; driven externally by innovation, diffusion, substitution, and syndication[cite: 4].
System Architecture: Inputs ➔ Conversion Sub-System ➔ Finished Outputs[cite: 4]
Feedback Control: Output Monitoring ➔ Signal Feedback ➔ Corrective Action[cite: 4]
