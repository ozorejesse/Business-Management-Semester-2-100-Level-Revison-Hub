AMS 104: Principles of Project Management

1. Week 1: Key Elements of Project Management

Introduces foundational project management concepts, key knowledge areas, and structural differences between temporary projects and ongoing business operations[cite: 1].

The Project Management Triangle (Triple Constraint)

  • Scope: The explicit work, features, and deliverables required to achieve project goals[cite: 1].
  • Time: The schedule, activity durations, and deadline constraints for project execution[cite: 1].
  • Cost: The allocated financial budget, resource costs, and expenditure limits[cite: 1].
  • Quality: Positioned at the core; directly affected by trade-offs among Scope, Time, and Cost[cite: 1].

Core PM Knowledge Areas

  • Scope Management: Defining deliverables, creating Work Breakdown Structures (WBS), and controlling scope creep[cite: 1].
  • Time Management: Activity sequencing, duration estimation, and building schedule models (Gantt/PERT)[cite: 1].
  • Cost Management: Budget estimation, resource cost allocation, and monitoring cost variances[cite: 1].
  • Integration Management: Unifying project processes, managing dependencies, and aligning work with strategic goals[cite: 1].

Projects vs. Operations

  • Projects: Temporary, unique, cross-functional, risk-laden, and driven by explicit deliverables[cite: 1].
  • Operations: Ongoing, repetitive, functional, predictable, and focused on sustaining business continuity[cite: 1].
Triple Constraint Relationship: Quality = f(Scope, Time, Cost)[cite: 1]
Project Objective: Deliver Unique Scope within Schedule & Budget[cite: 1]

2. Week 2: Benefits and Challenges of Project Management

Explores how structured project management creates organizational value and provides frameworks to overcome common project failure modes[cite: 2].

Core Organizational Benefits

  • Higher Success Rates: Systematic alignment of project outcomes with enterprise business strategy[cite: 2].
  • Optimized Resource Allocation: Efficient utilization of staff, budget, and physical equipment[cite: 2].
  • Proactive Risk Mitigation: Early identification and mitigation of threats to protect timelines[cite: 2].

Execution Challenges & Mitigation Strategies

  • Scope Creep: Uncontrolled scope growth managed via formal change request reviews and baseline control[cite: 2].
  • Resource Constraints: Overcoming staff/material shortages through resource leveling and capacity planning[cite: 2].
  • Communication Breakdown: Preventing misalignment using structured stakeholder communication matrixes[cite: 2].

Real-World Case Insights

  • The Greenway Complex (Construction): Overcame strict environmental rules and resource bottlenecks via risk response planning and stakeholder engagement[cite: 2].
  • Stellar App (Software): Navigated evolving specs and launch deadlines using Agile development, sprint iterations, and user testing[cite: 2].
Scope Creep Solution: Formal Change Control Board (CCB) Approval[cite: 2]
Agile Value: Iterative Sprints + Rapid User Feedback = Reduced Market Risk[cite: 2]

3. Week 3: Phases of the Project Delivery Process

Details the end-to-end project execution lifecycle, using an event management scenario (XYZ Music Festival) as a practical guide[cite: 3].

The 7 Sequential Delivery Phases

  • 1. Planning & Scheduling: Setting scope baselines, defining WBS tasks, and mapping milestone schedules[cite: 3].
  • 2. Execution & Monitoring: Coordinating teams, managing supplier logistics, and tracking status against plan[cite: 3].
  • 3. Risk Management: Assessing likelihood/impact of threats (weather, artist cancellations) and setting contingencies[cite: 3].
  • 4. Communication Management: Maintaining aligned reporting channels across artists, vendors, sponsors, and fans[cite: 3].
  • 5. Quality Management: Enforcing quality assurance checks for audio equipment, safety, and customer experience[cite: 3].
  • 6. Change Management: Assessing and approving scope change requests without disrupting overall budget[cite: 3].
  • 7. Project Closing: Conducting post-project reviews, vendor financial sign-offs, and archiving lessons learned[cite: 3].
Delivery Lifecycle Flow:
Initiation/Planning $\longrightarrow$ Execution & Monitoring $\longrightarrow$ Control (Risk/Quality/Change) $\longrightarrow$ Closing[cite: 3]

4. Week 4: Project Management Technology and Software

Examines modern digital tooling and enterprise technology stacks applied to large-scale infrastructure projects (e.g., Hospital Expansion Project)[cite: 4].

Software Categories & Industry Standard Tools

  • Scheduling & Task Management: Microsoft Project, Asana, Trello, Monday.com — Gantt charts, network diagrams, dependencies[cite: 4].
  • Communication & Collaboration: Microsoft Teams, Slack, Basecamp — Centralized messaging and real-time document sharing[cite: 4].
  • Risk & Quality Management: RiskyProject, Smartsheet, Zoho Projects — Risk matrixes, issue logs, and audit compliance[cite: 4].
  • Budgeting & Financial Management: QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks — Purchase order tracking, cost variance monitoring, and financial reports[cite: 4].
  • Performance Reporting & BI: Power BI, Tableau — Interactive dashboards tracking Earned Value (EV), KPIs, and progress[cite: 4].

Enterprise Tooling Architecture

  • Integrations & Mobility: Direct API connections with enterprise ERPs and mobile field logging capability[cite: 4].
  • Security Controls: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), end-to-end data encryption, and disaster recovery automated backups[cite: 4].
Enterprise PM Stack:
Schedule (MS Project) + Collaboration (Teams) + Financials (QuickBooks) + BI Dashboards (Power BI)[cite: 4]
